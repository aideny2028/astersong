AsterSong

Deterministic sonification of astronomical events: fixed, documented mappings, no generative audio.

github.com/aideny2028/astersong

Larger magnitude means fainter: +6 is roughly the naked-eye limit.

The whole decline is compressed into this many seconds by one linear factor.

How this works

Apparent magnitude is already a logarithm of flux (the Pogson scale), and pitch perception is a logarithm of frequency, so a mapping linear in magnitude and logarithmic in frequency makes equal magnitude steps equal musical intervals; brighter reads higher, and the brightest sample maps to 1760 Hz, the dimmest to 220 Hz. This page implements exactly the same formula as the AsterSong Python package, including its fades and sample rate, with no randomness anywhere: for a strictly linear decline the glide is always the full 1760 Hz to 220 Hz, so the magnitude sliders set the documented scale (semitones per magnitude) and the duration slider sets the documented compression factor, which the readout shows. Gallery audio, when it exists, will be rendered by the package itself; nothing here is generated by AI audio models.
