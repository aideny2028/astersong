Open source · historical astronomy · audio

Xuanji璇玑

A Python library that turns a light curve or a dated sky-record into sound. It maps the numbers you already have to pitch, tempo, and stereo position by fixed rules, so one record always returns the same audio. It does not compose, and it does not fill gaps. The tool interprets the data; it does not author it.

What the library is for

01

It reads a record and returns audio, deterministically

Give it a light curve, an eclipse timing, or a night-by-night comet track. The same input always produces the same waveform. No model guesses at missing nights, and nothing is invented between the data points.

02

It listens to change that a plot flattens

A supernova fading over two years, a totality that arrives as silence, a dark grain crossing the Sun: these read as slow curves on a page. As pitch and tempo they are easier to compare across events, and the ear catches a break the eye skims past.

03

Every event is cross-identified, with the uncertainty kept

Each curated record names its primary source (a Chinese court chronicle, a treatise), its modern cross-identification, and how firm that link is. A debated remnant stays labelled debated. The archive that supplies the records is a separate project; the library is its playback engine.

04

It is small, tested, and cite-able

Three mapping rules, a light-curve type, a sky-path type, and a plotter. Pinned dependencies, a test suite, and a Zenodo deposit with a DOI. You can read the whole thing in an afternoon.

Featured record · XJ-1054

The guest star of 1054, made audible

Song-dynasty court astronomers logged a 客星 (guest star) near Tianguan on 4 July 1054. It stayed visible in daylight for about 23 days and at night for roughly two years. Modern astronomy cross-identifies that record with the Crab Nebula and its pulsar, in Taurus.

Xuanji reconstructs the decline as a light curve and maps it to a falling pitch. What you hear is not a tune. It is the record itself, resampled into a sense the record was never written for.

The three rules

How a record becomes sound

Three fixed, documented mappings do all the work. There are no learned parameters and no randomness. Read the full model on the Mapping page.

brightnessapparent magnitude
pitchbrighter reads higher
timedays / nights / months
temporecord time to playback
sky positionazimuth / declination
stereowhere it stood in the sky
deterministic · no learned weights · same record in, same audio out

Built on the scientific-Python stack

Astropy
coordinates, precession, and time scales, so a record's position is placed in the sky of its own date.
NumPy · SciPy
the light-curve arrays and the resampling that turns record-time into audio-rate samples.
soundfile
writes WAV and FLAC. Playback and figures use only the returned array, nothing hidden.
Xuanji Archive
the sibling project that supplies the curated, cross-identified records this engine plays.
Sonification is a reading of the data, not a replacement for it. The judgement of what a record means stays with the person. Design principle, Xuanji
